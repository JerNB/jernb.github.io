Why I Still Listen to Zhang Yusheng

Jul 11, 2026 · Music, Essay

我为什么还在听张雨生

如果要认真写这个公众号的第一篇文章，我想我还是会从张雨生写起。

不是因为他最适合开篇，也不是因为我觉得自己已经足够了解他。反之，我总觉得张雨生是那种越听越觉得自己还没听懂的人。

张雨生是我最喜欢的华语创作型乐手。他拥有天赐的唱腔，他的高音太特别了，干净、亮、穿透力很强，好像不怎么需要修饰，就能把情绪直接抬到很高的地方。但我最欣赏他的并不是他的嗓音，而是他的创作、编曲能力。

说起张雨生，我们不自觉地会想起《大海》《我的未来不是梦》这样的经典之作，但我今天想分享一下我的三首最爱。

灵光

我想写的第一首，是《灵光》。

我很喜欢这个名字。“灵光”不是一种稳定的东西，它不是结论，也不是答案，更像某一瞬间突然亮起来的念头。它来的时候很快，消失的时候也很快。很多创作好像就是靠这种东西撑住的：你可能准备了很久，听了很多音乐，读了很多书，想了很多问题，但真正让你开始动笔、开始唱、开始做一首歌的，往往只是那一瞬间的闪光。

这首歌给我的感觉，就是张雨生把这种“闪一下”的东西真的写进了歌里。它不是一首特别规整的歌，也不是那种你听第一遍就会马上记住副歌的歌。它更像一个人在脑子里不断连接东西：一些旋律，一些古典音乐的影子，一些突然冒出来的念头。

我喜欢歌里的一句歌词：“如丝如缕的情爱 被精确的 Quaintize，至情至性的神采 也被 Digital 取代，那回眸一笑的人啊 依然使我情不自禁。” 回看现在，人工智能发展得如此迅速，人们越发强调“物理 AI”这个概念。因为除了血肉，一些 AI 看似慢慢拥有了人类的情感，不禁让我开始反思：当技术不断逼近甚至模拟人的感受时，我们是否也在不知不觉中，把真正属于人的那部分，那些无法被量化、无法被复制的情绪与灵魂，交给了机器去定义？

或许技术可以模仿情感，但无法真正经历人的成长与内心挣扎。那些片刻的犹豫和瞬间的灵光，正是人之为人的独特之处。所以无论技术如何发展，我们仍需要回到自身，去感受那些不完美却真实的情感。真正打动人的，从来不是被优化的表达，而是带着温度的真实本身。

永公街的街长

第二首是《永公街的街长》。

我特别喜欢这首歌，如果要定义一个曲风，我会说是前卫摇滚。它有叙事，有口白，有摇滚，也有很戏剧化的推进。它不像让你听一首歌，更像是请你坐下来听一个人、一条街、一个城市的角落。

他没有站在高处俯视这个人物，也没有急着替他解释什么。张雨生只是把目光停留得足够久，用了十分钟的复杂编曲去讲好一个小人物的故事。《永公街的街长》本来就不是一首适合用流行歌标准衡量的作品，它更接近一篇用声音写成的人物特写，不论是开头的戏腔还是后面的唱腔。它的庞大并非为了炫技，而是因为这个人物值得被如此认真地对待。

张雨生愿意把音乐的空间让给一个通常不会成为主角的人，让他的生命经验慢慢显现出来。这首歌让我看到张雨生身上很珍贵的一种宽阔：他的创作可以越过个人情绪，去注视更远、更复杂的现实。那些容易被忽略的人和事，到了他的音乐里，也可以拥有自己的重量。

河

第三首是《河》。

如果说《永公街的街长》是向外看的，看到城市和人群里的边缘处，那《河》对我来说就是向内流的。它很美，但不是那种轻松的美。它有一种很宽、很深、慢慢把人卷进去的力量。歌名只有一个字，“河”，但这个字本身就很厉害。河不是静止的，它一直在流；河也不是完全透明的，你看得到表面，却不知道下面藏着什么。

我更愿意把《河》看成一首诗。它的歌词太美，也太诗意了。张雨生的咬字非常清晰，声母韵母都交代得很到位，可你还是会有一种“听不懂”的感觉。因为他的遣词造句太特殊、太文艺，你很难猜到下一句歌词会落在哪里。也正因为这样，《河》留下了很大的解释空间：你可以把它读成爱情，也可以读成生命本身的流动。听的时候，我经常不是先理解歌词，而是先被它的空间感带走。好像一开始只是站在岸边，后来越听越往水里走，最后发现自己已经被卷进去了。

既然整首歌写得像一首诗，那么它的推进也有一点“赋比兴”的感觉。前面那些诗意、缓慢的段落并不急着把感情说透，而是让情绪一点点积蓄。直到最后，吉他突然冲出来，前面一直压着的东西才终于爆发。那段摇滚不是为了炫技，而是让整首歌突然有了身体，有了重量，也有了撕裂感，像一条河终于冲破水面。我觉得，张雨生其实一直是一个摇滚歌手。他不是因为用了电吉他才摇滚，而是因为他的很多歌都有一种“不愿意只停在安全位置”的冲动。

写在最后

这篇文章其实没有想讲完整张专辑，也没有想给张雨生下一个结论。我只是想聊三首歌，和三个让我停下来重新听他的瞬间。

如果一定要说它们有什么共同点，大概是：它们都让我觉得，张雨生不应该只被记住成“唱《大海》的那个人”。他会写《灵光》，会写《永公街的街长》，会写《河》。他的音乐可以像小说、像剧场、像城市观察，也可以是一场摇滚演出。

我喜欢张雨生，大概就是因为这个。

而如果要用这篇文章作为这个公众号的开头，我想说的其实也很简单：

我想在这里写音乐，不只是为了推荐几首好听的歌。我也想记录那些让我停下来、让我重新听、让我觉得“原来华语音乐可以这样”的瞬间。

张雨生不仅是其中第一个，也是我最喜欢的一位创作者。

也正因为这样，我到现在，还是愿意一遍一遍地听。

Why I Still Listen to Zhang Yusheng

If I were to write the first real essay for this account, I think I would still begin with Zhang Yusheng.

Not because he is the most obvious place to start, and not because I feel I understand him well enough. Quite the opposite. I have always felt that Zhang Yusheng is the kind of musician who makes me feel, the more I listen, that I still have not fully understood him.

Zhang Yusheng is my favorite singer-songwriter in Mandopop. He had a voice that felt almost gifted from above. His high notes were so distinctive: clean, bright, penetrating, as if they needed very little decoration to lift emotion straight into a higher place. But what I admire most is not his voice. It is his writing and arranging.

When people talk about Zhang Yusheng, they naturally think of classics like The Sea and My Future Is Not a Dream. But today, I want to write about my three favorites.

Ling Guang

The first song I want to write about is Ling Guang.

I really like the title. "Ling guang," a flash of inspiration, is not something stable. It is not a conclusion, nor an answer. It is more like a thought that suddenly lights up for a moment. It arrives quickly and disappears just as quickly. So much creation seems to be held up by this kind of thing: you may have prepared for a long time, listened to a lot of music, read many books, and thought through many questions, but what truly makes you begin writing, singing, or making a song is often only that instant of brightness.

What this song gives me is the feeling that Zhang Yusheng really wrote that sudden flash into the music. It is not an especially regular song, nor the kind of song whose chorus you immediately remember after the first listen. It feels more like a mind constantly making connections: fragments of melody, shadows of classical music, ideas that suddenly surface.

There is one lyric I especially like: "Threadlike strands of love are precisely quaintized; the truest human radiance is replaced by the digital; yet the person who turns back with a smile still leaves me helplessly moved." Looking back from the present, as artificial intelligence develops so rapidly and people increasingly emphasize the idea of "physical AI," I cannot help but reflect on this: beyond flesh and blood, some forms of AI seem to be slowly acquiring something like human emotion. When technology keeps approaching, and even imitating, human feeling, are we also unknowingly handing over the truly human part of ourselves, the emotions and souls that cannot be quantified or copied, for machines to define?

Perhaps technology can imitate emotion, but it cannot truly live through human growth and inner struggle. Those brief hesitations and sudden flashes of inspiration are exactly what make us human. No matter how technology develops, we still need to return to ourselves and feel emotions that are imperfect but real. What truly moves people has never been optimized expression. It is reality itself, carrying warmth.

The Street Chief of Yonggong Street

The second song is The Street Chief of Yonggong Street.

I love this song. If I had to define its genre, I would call it progressive rock. It has narrative, spoken passages, rock, and a highly dramatic sense of progression. It does not feel like it is simply asking you to listen to a song. It feels more like it invites you to sit down and listen to a person, a street, a corner of a city.

He does not look down on this character from above, nor does he rush to explain him. Zhang Yusheng simply lets his gaze stay long enough, using ten minutes of complex arrangement to tell the story of an ordinary person well. The Street Chief of Yonggong Street was never a work meant to be measured by the standards of a pop song. It is closer to a character profile written in sound, from the theatrical vocal style at the beginning to the singing that follows. Its scale is not there to show off technique. It is there because this person deserves to be treated with such seriousness.

Zhang Yusheng was willing to give musical space to someone who usually would not become the protagonist, letting that person's life experience slowly emerge. This song shows me a precious breadth in Zhang Yusheng: his writing can move beyond personal emotion and look toward a farther, more complicated reality. The people and things that are easy to overlook can, in his music, carry their own weight.

River

The third song is River.

If The Street Chief of Yonggong Street looks outward, toward the edges of the city and the crowd, then River, for me, flows inward. It is beautiful, but not in an easy way. It has a wide, deep force that slowly pulls you in. The title is only one character, "river," but that character already carries power. A river is not still; it keeps flowing. A river is not completely transparent either. You can see the surface, but you do not know what is hidden underneath.

I prefer to read River as a poem. Its lyrics are too beautiful, too poetic. Zhang Yusheng's articulation is extremely clear; every consonant and vowel is delivered carefully. And yet you still feel that you do not quite understand it. His wording and sentence structure are too unusual, too literary, and it is difficult to guess where the next line will land. Precisely because of that, River leaves a wide space for interpretation: you can read it as love, or as the movement of life itself. When I listen, I often do not understand the lyrics first. I am first taken away by its sense of space. It is as if I start by standing on the bank, then keep walking into the water as I listen, until I realize I have already been swept in.

Since the whole song is written like a poem, its progression also carries something like the classical Chinese movement of evocation, comparison, and direct expression. The poetic, slow sections at the beginning are not in a hurry to explain the emotion. They let it accumulate little by little. Then, at the end, the guitar suddenly rushes out, and everything that had been held down finally erupts. That rock section is not there to show off technique. It gives the whole song a body, a weight, and a feeling of rupture, as if a river has finally broken through the surface. I think Zhang Yusheng was always a rock musician. Not because he used electric guitar, but because so many of his songs carry an impulse that refuses to remain in a safe position.

At the End

This essay is not trying to discuss a whole album, nor is it trying to draw a conclusion about Zhang Yusheng. I only wanted to talk about three songs, and three moments that made me stop and listen to him again.

If they have anything in common, it is probably this: they all make me feel that Zhang Yusheng should not be remembered only as "the person who sang The Sea." He could write Ling Guang, The Street Chief of Yonggong Street, and River. His music can be like a novel, like theater, like urban observation, and also like a rock performance.

I think this is why I like Zhang Yusheng.

And if I were to use this essay as the beginning of this account, what I want to say is actually very simple:

I want to write about music here not only to recommend a few good songs. I also want to record the moments that make me stop, listen again, and feel: so Mandopop can be like this.

Zhang Yusheng is not only the first of those moments. He is also my favorite creator among them.

And that is why, even now, I am still willing to listen again and again.
